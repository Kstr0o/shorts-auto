What if all the ice on Earth melted?
Sea level would rise about seventy meters.
Almost all that ice sits in two places: Antarctica and Greenland.
Antarctica alone holds enough ice to raise the sea about fifty eight meters.
Much of London, New York and Venice would end up underwater.
And hundreds of millions of people would have to move inland.
But relax: melting it all would take thousands of years.
Still, the sea is already rising about four millimeters a year.
And one detail: floating ice doesn't raise the sea when it melts, but ice on land does.
