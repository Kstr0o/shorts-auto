Why do bridges have metal teeth in the road?
Because bridges are alive. They grow and shrink every day.
Heat makes steel and concrete expand. Cold makes them shrink.
A long bridge can grow by more than a foot in summer.
Without room to move, the road would push against itself and crack.
So engineers leave gaps. Those teeth are called expansion joints.
They let the bridge breathe, and cars still drive over smoothly.
That bump you feel? That is the bridge stretching.
Have you ever felt it?
