Why is there gravel under every train track?
It's called ballast, and it does four jobs.
First, it spreads the weight of a train, which can reach thousands of tons.
Second, it holds the sleepers in place.
Third, it lets rainwater drain away fast, so the track never turns to mud.
Fourth, it soaks up vibration, so the ride is smoother and quieter.
The stones are sharp and angular on purpose, so they lock together.
Round pebbles would roll and slide, and the track would slowly drift.
That's why nobody builds a railway on beach pebbles.
