Why do big ships have a giant bulb under the front?
It looks like a mistake. It saves millions of dollars.
A ship pushing through water makes a wave at the bow.
That wave takes energy. And energy is fuel.
The bulb makes its own wave, a little ahead of the ship.
The two waves meet at just the right moment and cancel each other out.
Less wave means less drag.
The result is around ten percent less fuel.
On a ship that burns tons of fuel a day, that adds up. Would you have guessed that?
