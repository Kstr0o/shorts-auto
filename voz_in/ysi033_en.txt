What if a dinosaur-killer asteroid hit us today?
It was a rock about ten kilometers wide, bigger than Mount Everest.
It hit Mexico sixty six million years ago.
The impact threw up so much dust that the sky stayed dark for months.
Without light the plants died, and so did three out of four species.
Today it would be just as bad, but there is one difference: we are watching.
Telescopes have already found over ninety percent of the giant rocks nearby, and none is heading our way.
And in twenty twenty two, the DART probe proved we can nudge an asteroid off course.
So no panic. Just keep looking up.
