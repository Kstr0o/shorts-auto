# Style Guide

## Rhythm
- Alternate short lines (1-6 words) with medium lines (10-18 words).
- Isolate key beats on their own line.
- Fragments are fine: "Static every day." "One number."
- Read aloud. If you run out of breath, split it.

## Voice
- Second person, present tense: "You pass the salt."
- Grounded and calm, even when the premise is wild. No hype words.
- Conversational transitions sparingly: "Here's the thing." "Think about that."
- Open and close with a wider perspective on human nature, time or modern life, but keep the middle personal.

## Vocabulary
- Concrete nouns and verbs: texture, temperature, weight, sound.
- Specifics: exact numbers, dates, objects ("9,431 days", "a cracked phone screen").
- Juxtapose everyday and extreme.

## Dialogue (human scene)
- Short, interrupted, unfinished. People do not speak in complete explanations.
- One line the viewer will remember.
- Let silence carry weight: "She waits. You don't answer."

## Avoid
- Passive voice.
- Unanchored sci-fi with no cost.
- Textbook summaries or stacked statistics.
- Narration that describes what the animation already shows.
- Filler ("basically", "essentially", "in conclusion").
- Tidy endings and moral summaries.
- Inventing facts or studies. Verify or leave out.
